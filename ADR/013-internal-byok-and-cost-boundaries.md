# ADR 013: Internal BYOK, configured providers, and measurable shared costs

Status: Accepted 2026-10-02. Shared analytical topology and subscription allocation remain open.

**Decision:** Internal testing and research can immediately use existing scoped BYOK credentials behind replaceable provider adapters. Configure provider/model explicitly per task role, with bounded calls and usage, cost, latency, and failure records. External pilots use operated credentials. Defer customer-facing BYOK, customer key-management flows, adaptive model routing, and commercial credit billing; none is a prerequisite for testing the desk.

**Shared work:** Share permitted source acquisition, normalization, dedupe, and source health. Introduce reusable event analysis when measured duplication warrants it and rights allow. Compare shared and independent model-generated macro-context construction during the pilot rather than silently selecting either topology. A future shared context holds versioned observations, hypotheses, counter-cases, and unresolved questions; private analyses can disagree. It is not a canonical model worldview.

**Budgets:** Bound individual investigations and aggregate activity over time, including fan-out and retries. Record shared acquisition/monitoring, shared analytical work, and private context/investigations separately. Prioritize urgent exposure, retain a path for capture and qualified urgent notices, and visibly queue/degrade deeper work when limits are reached. Operational exhaustion cannot establish event irrelevance.

**Commercial direction:** Managed subscriptions provide the integrated desk; economical model routing can contribute to margin. Shared funding, premium private-investigation allowances, and subscription allocation percentages require measured workload and commercial modelling. No shared-context tax, BYOK platform fee, or customer credit formula has been selected. Data, storage, hosting, retry, and support costs remain relevant alongside inference prices.

**Why:** Existing keys make internal research quick without committing the product to public BYOK. Provider independence preserves experimentation and later routing economics. Cost per useful assessment is more informative than an advertised token discount; model/provider differences alone do not require independent evidence pipelines.

**Rejected:** Implementing a complete routing/billing product before research; vendor lock-in based on advertised discounts; treating pooled provider credits as interchangeable; silently deducting unpredictable shared costs from private credits; private-data leakage through shared caches or analyses.

**Consequence:** Log model/configuration versions, units, retries, and estimated versus billed spend without exposing secrets. Enforce source entitlements and privacy before storage, model processing, reuse, and display. Incrementally update context from evidence rather than rebuild the macro environment on every event. Use the paper pilot to measure duplicate work and useful shared-analysis opportunities; do not claim a selected global analytical topology or billing allocation.
